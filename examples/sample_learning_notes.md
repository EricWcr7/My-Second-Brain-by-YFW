# Learning Notes: Active Recall and Spaced Practice

## Active recall

Active recall means trying to retrieve an idea from memory before looking at the
answer. A short question, a blank-page explanation, or a practice problem can all
create a retrieval attempt. The important step is comparing the attempt with the
source and correcting gaps.

## Spaced practice

Spaced practice revisits material across multiple sessions instead of grouping
all review into one sitting. The interval can grow when recall is easy and shrink
when a concept remains difficult.

## A simple study loop

1. Write one specific question for each important idea.
2. Answer without opening the notes.
3. Check the answer and record the missing detail.
4. Schedule another attempt after a delay.
5. Connect related ideas so later questions require comparison and transfer.

Active recall determines what can be retrieved now; spaced practice determines
when to try again. Used together, they turn review into repeated, evidence-based
practice rather than rereading.
